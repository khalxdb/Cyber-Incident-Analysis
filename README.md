# Cyber Incident Analysis

A university project examining two ransomware incidents: the 2021 attack on Ireland’s Health Service Executive (HSE) and the 2024 attack on Change Healthcare in the United States.

The project explores how each attack developed, the weaknesses involved and the measures that could reduce the likelihood or impact of similar incidents.

## Project Objectives

- Reconstruct attack pathways using publicly available evidence.
- Represent attacker objectives and actions through AND/OR attack trees.
- Compare the two incidents and identify shared patterns.
- Assess risks and propose security recommendations.
- Distinguish confirmed findings from assumptions and missing information.

## Approach

### Evidence Review

Reviewed published incident reports, organisational statements and government sources. Findings were linked to supporting evidence, with uncertainty identified where details were unavailable.

### Attack Tree Development

Created manual attack trees for each incident and compared them with AI-generated versions. Developed a synthesised tree to bring together common attack pathways while retaining incident-specific differences.

### Risk Assessment and Recommendations

Developed a risk and recommendation matrix connecting identified weaknesses with potential impacts and proposed security controls.

## Limitations

This analysis relies on publicly available information. It does not involve access to either organisation’s internal systems, original forensic images or confidential investigation records.

Where evidence was incomplete, findings were marked as uncertain rather than treated as established facts. The attack trees are analytical representations and should not be interpreted as complete forensic reconstructions.

## AI Use

ChatGPT was used to generate attack trees for comparison with manually developed versions. AI-generated content was critically reviewed against the available evidence and was not treated as an authoritative source.
